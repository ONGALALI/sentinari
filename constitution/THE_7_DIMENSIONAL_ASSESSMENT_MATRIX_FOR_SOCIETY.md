### 4.1 The Single-Dimension Potency Formulation
The baseline sustainability impact $G_i(x_i)$ of any dimension $i$ operating at level $x_i \in [0, 10]$ is modeled as a **Gaussian Bell Curve**:
$$G_i(x_i) = A_i \cdot \exp\left(-\frac{(x_i - \mu_i)^2}{2\sigma_i^2}\right)$$

Where:
* $x_i$: The measured operational level of dimension $i$ along the standardized $[0, 10]$ sliding scale.
* $\mu_i$ (The Sweet Spot): The optimal civilizational equilibrium point.
* $\sigma_i$ (Tolerance / Variance): How sensitive society is to deviations from the sweet spot.
* $A_i$ (Amplitude): The maximum potency / weight of that dimension.

### 4.2 The Normalized Sustainability Score Function $S_i(x_i)$
To normalize each dimension to a standard $[0.0, 1.0]$ efficiency interval:
$$S_i(x_i) = \frac{G_i(x_i)}{A_i} = \exp\left(-\frac{(x_i - \mu_i)^2}{2\sigma_i^2}\right)$$
When a dimension operates exactly at its sweet spot ($x_i = \mu_i$), $S_i = 1.0$. As it deviates toward deficiency ($x_i \to 0$) or dangerous excess ($x_i \to 10$), the score drops exponentially.

1) The Global Risk Index

2025/2026 Global Risk Landscape & Systemic Pillars (163 Countries)

Multi-pillar quantitative assessment of global systemic risk across 163 countries and 109 indicators—integrating data from The World Bank, IMF, UN, WHO, and SIPRI to measure national vulnerability and resilience.

  • ▸8 Core Risk Pillars: Quality of Governance, Health & Human Security, Economic Stability, Gender Inequality, Environmental Vulnerabilities, Education & Skills, Business Resilience, and Exogenous Threats.
  • ▸Systemic Propagation: Tracks how fiscal debt, inflation, regime fragility, and environmental shocks compound across borders.

2) The Global Catastrophic Index

Navigating Risk in an Uncertain Future • Global Governance Forum

Authored by Augusto Lopez-Claros & John Miller (with Aminah Malik & Ben Sweeney). Synthesizes nearly 20,000 data points across 163 nations (99% of global GDP, 98% of global population) to rank catastrophic risk exposure.

  • ▸Most vs. Least At-Risk: Contrasts highest-vulnerability states (Yemen 63.8, Afghanistan 62.4, Haiti 61.1, South Sudan 60.7, Sudan 60.0) with highest-resilience democracies (Norway 16.1, Denmark 16.7, Sweden 17.3).
  • ▸7 Areas to Watch: Conflict spillover, energy dependencies, nuclear proliferation, Russo-Ukrainian trajectory, sovereign debt crises, and unchecked AI risks.

3) The Lancet Report

The Lancet Commission on 21st Century Global Threats to Health (2025–2100)

Examining hundreds of threats across 204 countries (co-chaired by Prof. Christopher JL Murray & Dr. Natalia Kanem), identifying 17 catastrophic threats to human survival and quantifying avoidable DALYs through 2100.

  • ▸Extinction-Level Threats: Malicious AI (up to 10.4B amenable DALYs averted by action) and nuclear conflict (133M deaths / 9.35B DALYs averted).
  • ▸15 Major Catastrophic Threats & 3-Part Agenda: Climate change (1.89B DALYs), pandemics, AMR, air pollution, obesity, and inequality — addressed via Build Readiness, Back Breakthroughs, and Track Threats.
